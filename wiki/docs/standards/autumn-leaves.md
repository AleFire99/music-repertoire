# Autumn Leaves

Originally a 1945 French chanson ("Les Feuilles Mortes", music by Joseph
Kosma, French lyrics by Jacques Prévert), with English lyrics later added by
Johnny Mercer. It entered the jazz repertoire in the 1950s and is one of the
most commonly called standards at jam sessions, largely because its changes
are a clean, repeating tour of diatonic ii–V–I motion in both a major key and
its relative minor.

## Form and changes

32 bars, AABC. In the classic Cm (or G minor) lead-sheet version:

- **A (8 bars):** ii–V–I–IV in the relative major (Cm7 – F7 – B♭maj7 – E♭maj7)
- **A (8 bars):** repeats the same progression
- **B (8 bars):** ii–V–i in the minor (Am7♭5 – D7 – Gm)
- **C (8 bars):** turnaround back to the top, often Am7♭5 – D7 – Gm – Gm

The whole tune is essentially two ii–V–I cycles glued together — major, then
its relative minor — which is why it's a standard first call for practicing
that progression in real musical context rather than in isolation.

## Playing notes

- Works well as a vehicle for practicing minor ii–V–i licks: the B section
  is the same two bars repeated, so it's an easy place to try a line twice
  with variation.
- Commonly taken up-tempo as a swing tune (Cannonball/Miles) or as a slow
  ballad (many vocal versions) — worth learning both feels since the tune
  gets called both ways.
- Bill Evans' Portrait in Jazz version reharmonizes the turnaround and is a
  good reference for a more modern comping approach.
